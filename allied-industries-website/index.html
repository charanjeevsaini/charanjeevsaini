What we manufacture

Five constructions, one cold‑heading floor

Rivets are classified by how they are built and set. Everything we make belongs to one of these five.

See the full range

01 / 05 · Construction

Solid rivets

One material, end to end.

Cold-headed from a single wire — solid silver alloy for contacts, ETP copper for conductors. The simplest construction and the strongest joint.

Built
Single metal, solid shank
Set by
Upsetting the whole tail
Used in
Low-current contacts, earth bonding
  • Solid Contact Rivet
  • Copper Rivet
2 types

02 / 05 · Construction

Semi-tubular rivets

A blind bore in the tail.

The shank end is drilled shallower than its diameter, so the tail rolls over instead of upsetting — about a quarter of a solid rivet's setting force, gentle on thin terminals and plastic housings.

Built
Solid head, bored shank end
Set by
Rolling the bored tail outward
Used in
Terminal fixing, switch assemblies
  • Semi-Tubular Rivet
1 type

03 / 05 · Construction

Composite contact rivets

Silver where it switches, copper everywhere else.

A silver-alloy facing is bonded to a copper body — on the head (bimetal) or on both ends (trimetal). Full contact performance with a fraction of the precious metal.

Built
Ag facing bonded to a Cu base
Facings
AgNi, AgCdO, AgSnO₂, Ag 99.9, hard gold
Used in
MCBs, relays, contactors
  • Bimetal Contact
  • Bimetal Radius-Head
  • Trimetal Contact
  • Straight Head
4 types

04 / 05 · Construction

Special-form rivets

Shaped to the carrier they sit in.

Heads trimmed with flats, formed to non-round profiles, doubled, or carried on two shanks — geometry that locks against rotation, bridges two contacts or spreads current over a wider face.

Built
Trimmed or formed heads, double head or shank
Solves
Anti-rotation, bridging, wide contact faces
Used in
Rotary switches, isolators, bridging contacts
  • Trimmed Rivet
  • Formed Rivet
  • Double Head
  • Double Shank
4 types

05 / 05 · Construction

Weldable contacts

No shank — welded straight on.

A contact facing on a weldable nickel, steel or CuNi base, resistance-welded to the carrier through projections or a flat back. No hole and no riveting step, built for automated lines.

Built
Ag facing on a weldable base
Set by
Projection or resistance welding
Used in
Appliance controls, automated assembly
  • Projection Weldable
  • Weldable Button
  • Disc Contact
3 types
