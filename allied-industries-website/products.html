    Guide

    Finishes, materials and contact alloys

    What each plating, base material and contact alloy buys you and what it costs you, so you can narrow a specification before you send a drawing.

    Plating compared
    PlatingWhat it buys youWatch out forTypical use
    TinExcellent solderability and low contact resistance when freshWhisker growth in thin bright deposits; creeps under sustained pressureConnectors, crimp terminals, PCB hardware
    NickelHard, wear resistant, and an effective diffusion barrierHigher contact resistance; needs wiping action to break its oxideUnderplate below silver, high-temperature parts
    SilverLowest contact resistance of the practical finishesTarnishes to sulphide in polluted air; soft, so it wearsSwitching contacts, relay and contactor faces
    ZincProtects steel galvanically even where scratched; very cheapNot a contact surface; white corrosion in humid storageStructural steel rivets, mechanical fasteners
    Base materials compared
    MaterialKey propertyTrade-offTypical use
    ETP copper (C11000)Highest conductivity; very ductile, so the shank sets without crackingLow shear strength; work hardens under heavy formingContact rivet bases, harness terminals, busbar joints
    Brass (CuZn)Stronger than copper and machines to a burr-free edgeA quarter to a third of copper's conductivity; dezincifies in damp serviceSemi-tubular rivets, switchgear hardware
    NickelResists oxidation hot; projection-welds reliablyMarkedly more expensive; low conductivityWeldable contact bases, harsh environments
    CuNiCopper's formability with better corrosion and weld behaviourConductivity well below pure copperWeldable backings, marine and humid service
    Steel (low carbon)Highest strength per unit cost; welds and plates readilyNot a contact material; rusts if the plating is breachedProjection-weld bases, structural rivets
    Contact alloys compared
    AlloyStrengthTrade-offTypical use
    Ag 99.9 (fine silver)Lowest contact resistance and highest conductivity availableSoft and welds readily; unsuited to inductive switchingLow-current signal contacts, thermostats
    AgNi 10Low contact resistance, free of restricted substances, cold forms wellMore prone to welding than oxide alloys at high inrushAppliance and automotive switches, general relays
    AgNi 15Harder than AgNi 10, with better erosion and anti-weld behaviourSlightly higher contact resistance; harder to headRelays and switches with heavier loads
    AgCdO 12Outstanding arc-erosion resistance and very low tendency to weldCadmium is restricted under RoHS; use depends on your exemptionLegacy or exemption-covered relays and contactors
    AgSnO₂ 12Excellent anti-weld under heavy inrush; erosion close to AgCdOHigher contact resistance so the contact runs hotter; harder to formContactors, automotive relays, high-inrush loads
    Hard gold platedDoes not tarnish; stable resistance at very low currentsThin and soft — for dry circuits, not for arcing loadsSignal and sensing contacts, low-level switching
    Taper 5° – 25° minimum, every size Profile F / R / SPH — flat, radius or spherical head Layer material AgCdO · AgNi · AgSnO · AgCu · Ag999 and others
